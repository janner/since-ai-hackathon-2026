For each notice the specialist answers three questions: can this satellite reach Finnish territory, does Finland use that band at all, and is the level over the ITU limit. Then comes a letter to the administration behind the filing, before the coordination window closes.

The solution

Four steps, every notice, nothing sampled

1 · Fetch

Reads the BR IFIC Access database directly with mdb-export. 17 tables, the input hash on every run. A scheduled check pulls the next package from ITU.

2 · Filter

Keeps space-to-Earth notices whose band overlaps a Finnish allocation and whose satellite is visible from Finland. Everything else leaves with a reason.

3 · Compute

Worst-case power flux density over the 552 points of Finland's border and territorial sea limit, against Table 21-4. Then the Finnish allocation: is the band a satellite downlink at all.

4 · Decide

A case card: source rows, calculation, the gap, the regulatory stage, what is missing, and one question for the other administration. A human decides.

The language model writes prose only. A numeric guard rejects any sentence whose number the card does not have.

What the card answers
Where. The worst point on Finland's border or territorial sea limit, with the elevation and the slant range behind the number.
How much. The upper-bound PFD, the Table 21-4 limit row it is measured against, and the margin between them.
Whether it matters here. The Finnish allocation, and the exact part of the band Finland has not given to satellite downlink.
What to do. The regulatory stage, the deadline, and the one question to the other administration. The specialist decides; the tool never sends anything.

What we did not build

The limits, stated on the card

  • The PFD is a screening upper bound. No I/N, no receiver noise figure, no bandwidth term: a case means the upper bound is over the limit, not that interference exists.
  • Beam geometry is empty in IFIC 3074, so the worst case is always assumed and the card asks the administration to confirm it.
  • Table 21-4 footnote conditions and the row's own service scope are not modelled. Both make the screen stricter, never looser, and a notice that cannot be computed is never called clear.
  • AP30 plan data, Article 22 EPFD and M15 are read as next steps, not as features.
  • Our labelled evaluation set is our own reading, not Traficom's: 16 of 21 agree. No accuracy claim against the customer follows from it.

What comes next

A pilot in three steps

1 · Three weeks

Match the limit row to the notice's service, model the Table 21-4 footnote conditions, and keep the table current. This removes the cases that are flagged only because the row was applied by band.

2 · One quarter

Run it inside Traficom's own OpenShift on every incoming IFIC, next to the manual pass, and compare the two lists. The funnel already runs there as a Data Science Pipeline.

3 · Then

Space-side trends across consecutive issues, AP30 plan data, and the CEPT/ECC DocDB tier. The same card and the same queue carry them.

Everything that runs in the cluster is in the repository, including the pipeline manifests and the test vectors. The laptop run is the primary demo, because it needs no infrastructure.
